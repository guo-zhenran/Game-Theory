A strategic game is a scenario where players make decisions independently and simultaneously. For perfect information, consider an experimental setting, every subject, anonymously sitting behind a computer, is shown a payoff matrix and asked to choose their strategy.
They won't be able to see the outcome until everyone makes their choice. To make a correct decision, they should care not only about their own choice, but also make decisions under other subjects' possible action, i.e., form a belief. 
To be more specific, we need to consider two things: how should one interact given opponents' action? And, what are opponents' possible actions?
We will formalize this idea soon after we give the definition of a strategic game.

\begin{definition}[Strategic Game]
    A strategic game is a 3-tuple $\langle N,\mathcal{A}, \{\preceq_i\}_{i\in N}  \rangle$. Everything is deined in the introduction part of the book. If every $\mathcal{A}_i$ is finite, we say the game is finite.
\end{definition}
