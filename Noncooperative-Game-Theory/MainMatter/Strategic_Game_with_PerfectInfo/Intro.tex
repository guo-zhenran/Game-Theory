Before getting into the definitions, we make clear what our goal is in strategic games as a player. As we assume players are rational, i.e., payoff seekers, we are solving a maximum problem. 
Given different game settings or solution concepts, there's slight difference in notation or technique. But, generally speaking, a player in a game should form a reasonable belief and make optimal decisions under such a belief.

In general, we consider that a player $i$ with the belief $(\mathcal{A}_{-i},\pi_i)$, where $\pi_i$ is a probability measure on $\mathcal{A}_{-i}$, is trying to maximize his/her payoff by choosing a strategy $\sigma_i\in \Delta(\mathcal{A}_i)$, i.e., 
\begin{equation}\label{equ: strategic_gameUMP}
    \max_{\sigma_i\in \Delta(\mathcal{A}_i)} \sum_{a_i\in \mathcal{A}_i}\sum_{a_{-i} \in \mathcal{A}_{-i}}\sigma_i (a_i) \pi_i (a_{-i})u_{i}(a_i,a_{-i}),
\end{equation}
\,and denote its solution the best response correspondence
\begin{equation}\label{equ: strategic_gameBR}
    BR_i(\pi_i) := \arg \max_{\sigma_i\in \Delta(\mathcal{A}_i)} \sum_{a_i\in \mathcal{A}_i}\sum_{a_{-i} \in \mathcal{A}_{-i}}\sigma_i (a_i) \pi_i (a_{-i})u_{i}(a_i,a_{-i}).
\end{equation}
As long as the belief is reasonable, a game is trivial as player can do their best by playing a strategy in the best-response correspondence. Thus, we'll first formalize the reasoning process and then turn to solve such a UMP.

For the complete information case, we'll first introduce several settings where belief is determined in different ways, and under such belief, how should players react.
In the following section, we introduce the concept of rationalizability, which concerns what belief is reasonable.
